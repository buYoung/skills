# Drafting and revision

Use this method to connect the selected document type's requirements with the actual text. It supplements that type's structure, operation boundaries, and validation sequence; it does not replace them or introduce new approval gates. Keep working questions and evidence notes internal unless the user requests them as deliverables.

## Establish completion conditions

Before drafting, identify the reader's starting knowledge and situation, the intended understanding or action, the requested coverage, and the applicable type-specific quality and validation criteria. Distinguish:

- Required content and checks: what the request and document type require for the permitted scope.
- Material unknowns: what remains unsupported or undecided, how it affects use or interpretation, and how the type requires it to be handled.
- Optional improvements: extra examples, supporting material, or presentation refinements that add value only after the required work is complete.

Reuse the selected type's review criteria rather than inventing another universal scorecard. For a focused revision, establish completion for the affected scope, not a new whole-document obligation. These conditions are working context, not a required extra section or file.

## Optional question-and-evidence outline

Use this when a new document or substantial rewrite has scattered material or an unclear outline. Skip it when an existing outline, contract inventory, chronology, or decision note already organizes the work adequately.

1. Derive the reader's necessary questions from their outcome and starting knowledge, the explicit request, and the supplied material. Repeated questions in supplied answers or notes can identify useful topics; do not automatically add an FAQ.
2. Merge redundant questions and remove those outside the requested scope. Retain material requirements even when the reader may not know to ask about them.
3. Order questions by dependencies and reader use. A reader may need an explanation, an ordered action, or non-linear lookup rather than a narrative sequence.
4. Place existing answers, evidence, examples, and counterevidence under the relevant questions. Preserve source, date or version when relevant, applicability, and whether a statement is confirmed, inferred, or unresolved. Several notes repeating one source are not independent corroboration.
5. Fill the actual gaps: missing explanations, transitions, necessary verification, or unresolved conflicts. Do not select only evidence supporting an anticipated conclusion. Follow [source-grounding.md](source-grounding.md) when claims require evidence.
6. Draft into the selected type's structure. Questions need not become headings, and the final document need not be a Q&A. Consolidate repeated answers while preserving conditions and exceptions at their appropriate owner.

Accept fragments, notes, questions, and supplied conversation records without requiring the user to prepare a polished manuscript. Ask only for missing experience, judgment, or facts that materially affect the document and cannot be recovered from available inputs. Do not invent personal experience or turn every working question into a user interview. Apply the type's existing missing-input rules.

Reuse is not permission to skip freshness or applicability checks. Keep a source's conditions attached when moving its explanation. Link to existing material when that serves the reader, but keep the requested document sufficiently complete for its intended use.

### Adapt to the document type

| Type | Role of questions and organizing authority |
| --- | --- |
| Explanatory overview or action guide | Questions can drive the outline, with prerequisites before dependent concepts or steps. Preserve observable outcomes where the guide requires them. |
| Analysis report or proposal | Use analytical or decision questions to organize evidence, including opposing findings and limitations. Derive the conclusion from evidence before writing the opening summary. |
| Reference or specification, policy or rules | Start from the authoritative inventory of items, obligations, conditions, and exceptions. Use questions to check completeness and usability; retain stable lookup keys and normative meaning. |
| Record or meeting notes | Preserve source-backed events, statements, decisions, disagreements, and chronology where it matters. Questions test reconstruction; they do not authorize interpretation or new decisions. |
| FDD or Design System Document | Reuse the existing decision note or owning-file/section structure. Map questions to it without creating a competing outline, decision authority, or validation process. |

## Review content in both directions

Inspect the actual draft against its inputs, not merely the intended outline. Follow the type-specific review procedure when one exists and use these two directions within it:

- Reader to text: can the intended reader locate and understand the answers needed to understand, act, decide, comply, recover, or reconstruct? Check prerequisites, explanation order, evidence, and useful observable results.
- Requirements and evidence to text: did the material requested content and supported facts, decisions, conditions, exceptions, and uncertainty survive without omission or changed meaning? Are additions supported or clearly qualified? In an existing document, compare the result with the original and authorized changes.

Answering every self-generated question does not establish completeness; the questions themselves may omit a required contract item or an inconvenient source fact. Repair content gaps, contradictions, unsupported certainty, and misplaced material before investing in sentence polish. Do not answer unresolved substantive choices just to complete the outline.

When reporting findings or seeking feedback, identify the passage, the relevant reader question or requirement, and the reason it is deficient. Ask a specific review question only when feedback is needed; do not require an additional feedback round for every document.

## Edit prose, then check meaning

Once the content is sound for the permitted scope, apply [human-readable-writing.md](human-readable-writing.md) to wording, transitions, repetition, terminology, and grammar. Its examples illustrate improvements constrained by the supplied evidence.

After editing, compare affected wording with its evidence or governing definition. A shorter sentence may unintentionally broaden the audience, remove an exception, promote observation to causation, or change permission into obligation. If meaning, certainty, conditions, or normative strength changed, return to content review for the affected passages and dependent references. Repeat structural checks when the selected type requires them after structural edits; do not restart unrelated whole-document work.

## Respect the requested operation

- Create or substantial rewrite: apply the content-then-prose sequence, using the question outline only when useful.
- Focused update or normalization: preserve the established contract and inspect the changed material plus related owners and dependencies. Do not expand this into an unrelated rewrite.
- Review or fact-check: inspect and report within scope without editing files or metadata. Prioritize substantive findings over wording issues; fact-checking need not include an unrelated style review.
- Mechanical correction: make the requested correction and check local meaning and exact text. Skip outline building and broad content revision.

## Finish at the right boundary

Finish when the requested scope and type-specific completion conditions are satisfied, material uncertainty is handled as that type requires, and only optional improvement remains. Preserve required nuance and evidence even when doing so makes the document longer.

A report can be complete when it explains why the evidence cannot settle a question; a record can be complete while faithfully recording an unresolved decision. An FDD with blocking design gaps or a partial Design System Document must retain the status and completion limits required by its own workflow. An unresolved marker alone neither proves completion nor makes every document incomplete.

Do not turn illustrative question counts, paragraph lengths, action durations, or publishing cadences into universal requirements. This workflow does not add publication scheduling, portfolio management, or recurring retrospective work.
