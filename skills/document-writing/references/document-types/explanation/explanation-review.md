# Reviewing an explanatory overview

## Reader test

A reader with the stated prior knowledge should be able to answer:

1. What is this?
2. Why does it matter?
3. How do the important parts relate?
4. What is a realistic example?
5. Where does the explanation stop applying?

## Findings to surface

- The opening delays or hides the main point.
- A term is used before it is defined.
- A section changes topic without a new heading or transition.
- The same idea is repeated without adding precision.
- Examples are absent where abstraction is high.
- Details are accurate but ordered around the author's research process.
- The artifact is actually a specification, guide, report, or FDD.

## Quality bar

Prefer a short coherent narrative over exhaustive coverage. Preserve nuance that changes the reader's mental model, and remove detail that only proves how much the author knows.
