# Record or meeting notes structure

## Default structure

1. Title
2. Date, time, location, or channel
3. Participants and roles
4. Purpose or context
5. Concise summary
6. Chronological or topic-based record
7. Decisions and agreements
8. Agreed action items, when present in the source or explicitly requested
9. Open questions and follow-up
10. Attachments or source links

## Responsibility boundaries

### Time and source coverage

Distinguish when an event occurred, when a statement or decision was made, and when the meeting was held. Put event times in the event record and statement or decision times with their attribution; meeting metadata contains only supported meeting dates and times. The first and last logged events establish source coverage, not meeting start and end. Label an unknown meeting time as unknown or omit that field.

### Record

Capture material statements, events, and evidence without turning them into recommendations.

### Decisions and agreements

Record what was actually agreed, by whom, and any effective condition. Do not infer consensus from silence.

### Action items

Preserve the agreed action even when its owner or due date is unknown. Include only known values and mark missing ownership or timing explicitly rather than inventing it or omitting the action.

## Ordering

Use chronology when sequence matters. Use topic grouping when discussion order would obscure meaning, while preserving timestamps or attribution needed for traceability.
